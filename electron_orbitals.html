Why is the B² (diamagnetic) term left out by default?

In a field B along z the electron's energy gains two magnetic terms: the linear Zeeman term mB/2 (the orbital magnet μ = −L/2 turning in the field) and the diamagnetic term B²ρ²/8, with ρ the distance from the field axis.

Why it is off: the linear term only shifts each |n,l,m⟩ level, so the familiar hydrogen orbitals stay exact stationary states and the app can evolve them analytically. The B² term does not: it couples l to l ± 2, so field-free orbitals stop being stationary. Up to 20 T it shifts every shell n ≤ 7 by less than half the bandwidth of a 400-cycle pulse, so leaving it out changes nothing you can see.

What it changes: the shift grows as B²⟨ρ²⟩/8 with ⟨ρ²⟩ ∝ n⁴, so big orbitals feel it first. It squeezes orbitals toward the field axis, mixes s with d (and p with f) within a shell, raises every level, and at very strong fields stretches states into cigars along z (the Landau regime of white dwarfs and neutron stars). The toy range shows the linear term alone at fields where real atoms look quite different.
